We did London in June, during a 35-degree heatwave, with a not-yet-
three-year-old. London has a reputation as the easiest vegan city in
Europe, and on paper it is: everything is labeled, every menu has
options, nobody blinks. In practice we found two big asterisks — one
about the food, one about getting anywhere with a stroller. Both below,
because that's what this blog is for.

## The food: hunt the independents

Here's the thing nobody tells you: London's vegan abundance is mostly
*chain* abundance. Finding authentic food that isn't part of a group
with fourteen locations takes actual work. These three made the work
worth it:

- **[Merkamo Vegan](https://maps.google.com/?q=Merkamo+Vegan+London)** —
  an amazing fully vegan Ethiopian restaurant with an owner so lovely we
  stayed talking long after the injera was gone. This is the meal we
  measure the trip by: generous, personal, completely unlike anything a
  chain will ever serve you.
- **[Tofu Vegan](https://maps.google.com/?q=Tofu+Vegan+Spitalfields+London)**
  (Spitalfields) — really nice Chinese food, and a properly good mapo
  tofu. The staff handed Luca a set of children's chopsticks with a
  rubber band holding them together, and our not-yet-three-year-old —
  to widespread astonishment, his own included — actually ate with
  them. A restaurant that engineers a toddler's finest motor-skills
  hour deserves your money.
- **[Mildreds](https://maps.google.com/?q=Mildreds+Soho+London)** (Soho)
  — the classy brunch. We deployed the nap-time trick: time the
  stroller nap for the reservation, roll in with a sleeping child, and
  enjoy a civilized meal like people in a previous life. Ten out of
  ten, will scheme again.

## The museums are free. Free!

Still slightly stunned by this. The
**[Natural History Museum](https://maps.google.com/?q=Natural+History+Museum+London)**,
the **[Science Museum](https://maps.google.com/?q=Science+Museum+London)**,
the **[British Museum](https://maps.google.com/?q=The+British+Museum+London)**
— all of them, entirely free, all air-conditioned, all excellent
toddler containment during a heatwave. You've read about them in ten
other blogs, so we'll just add: with a small child, "free" means you
can leave after 90 minutes without doing cost-per-hour math, and that
changes everything.

## The heatwave headquarters

**[Buckhill Playground](https://maps.google.com/?q=Buckhill+Playground+London)**
(Kensington Gardens) saved us: a free playground with a small pond,
which at 35 degrees is not a landscaping detail but critical
infrastructure. Bring a change of clothes. Accept the inevitable.

## The stroller vs. the Underground

Now the honest part. We were genuinely shocked by how inaccessible
London is with a stroller. Large parts of the Tube involve flights of
stairs with no lift in sight, which means carrying a loaded stroller
up them, or the genuinely dangerous move of balancing it on an
escalator — we did both, and endorse neither.

If you're coming with wheels: check the step-free access map before
you build your day, favor buses (level boarding, and the front seats
upstairs are a toddler amusement ride in themselves), and budget extra
time for everything. London, you're a wonderful city with free
museums; sort your stations out.

## Also on our list

Our saves also included
**[Unity Diner](https://maps.google.com/?q=Unity+Diner+London)** and
**[Dauns](https://maps.google.com/?q=Dauns+London)** — both come with

## The verdict

**Difficulty: 3/5.** The food is everywhere but the *good* food takes
hunting, the museums are a free air-conditioned miracle, and the
Underground is the boss fight. Come in summer for the parks, master
the nap-time trick, and pack biceps for the stairs.
