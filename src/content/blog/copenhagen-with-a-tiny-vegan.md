Let's address it upfront: Copenhagen is expensive. Not "hmm, pricey"
expensive — *check the receipt twice, no that's correct* expensive. Now
that we've grieved together, the good news: it's also one of the easiest,
kindest, best-fed cities we've done with a small vegan human, and with a
little strategy you can have a glorious time without remortgaging.

## Where we stayed

**[Scandic Kødbyen](https://maps.google.com/?q=Scandic+K%C3%B8dbyen+Copenhagen)**,
right by the Meatpacking District — yes, the vegan family stayed in the
Meatpacking District, and no, we will not be taking questions. It's a
great base: former industrial halls now full of food and galleries,
walkable to the center, and mercifully un-touristy in the mornings.

## The pastry incident

**[Kaf](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kaf+Copenhagen)** is an all-vegan
café where the pastry counter requires a moment of silence. Everything
is vegan, everything looks like it won a design award, and the coffee
holds its own in a city that takes coffee personally.

For the record of history: Luca ate **two cardamom buns**. Then he
requested to "taste" ours, which is how two adults ended up splitting
one bun between them like wartime rationing while the smallest member
of the party finished his second. His cardamom bun era, which began in
Prague, shows no signs of slowing.

## The rest of the eating

- **[DEJ](https://maps.google.com/?q=DEJ+pizza+Copenhagen)** — the
  vegan pizza of the trip, and a decidedly classy one: this is pizza
  that arrives looking like it has opinions about natural wine.
  Toddler-tested, parent-savored, nobody flinched at the arugula
  redistribution program happening at seat three.
- **[Vital Foods](https://maps.google.com/?q=Vital+Foods+Copenhagen)** —
  the brunch stop: fully vegan, casual, the kind of bagels-and-good-things
  menu that keeps both a toddler and his parents quietly occupied for an
  hour.

## The hummus chapter

**[Gaza Grill](https://maps.google.com/?q=Gaza+Grill+Copenhagen)**
deserves its own heading, because this was — and we do not say this
lightly — **the best hummus in Europe**. Silky, warm, properly dressed,
the kind of hummus that makes you re-evaluate every supermarket tub
you've ever accepted into your life. A toddler-approved protein
delivery system and a pilgrimage site for the rest of us.

## Burning energy without burning money

- **[DGI Byen](https://maps.google.com/?q=DGI+Byen+Copenhagen)** — a
  big sports complex where we played table tennis for a genuinely fair
  price, which in Copenhagen feels like winning the lottery. Perfect
  rainy-day energy burn for the whole family.
- **[The Planetarium](https://maps.google.com/?q=Planetarium+Copenhagen)**
  — an awesome experience, full stop. The building alone (that great
  tilted cylinder on the lake) is worth the walk, and inside, the dome
  show had all three of us equally hypnotized.
- **[Skydebanehaven](https://maps.google.com/?q=Skydebanehaven+Absalonsgade+12+Copenhagen)**
  — a really lovely outdoor playground in Vesterbro, entered through
  what appears to be a full fairytale castle wall, because Copenhagen
  doesn't do anything halfway. Cost of admission: zero kroner, the
  city's rarest price.

![The turreted red-brick castle wall at the entrance to Skydebanehaven playground in Vesterbro, Copenhagen](/images/copenhagen/skydebanehaven.jpg)

Copenhagen's greatest hits don't need our retelling:
**[Tivoli Gardens](https://maps.google.com/?q=Tivoli+Gardens+Copenhagen)**
(and the **[LEGO Store](https://maps.google.com/?q=LEGO+Store+Tivoli+Gardens+Copenhagen)**
inside it), the
**[Experimentarium](https://maps.google.com/?q=Experimentarium+Copenhagen)**,

bowling at **[DGI City Bowling](https://maps.google.com/?q=DGI+City+Bowling+Copenhagen)**,
curry at **[Café Munk](https://maps.google.com/?q=Caf%C3%A9+Munk+Copenhagen)**,
and flat whites at
**[norange coffee roasters](https://maps.google.com/?q=norange+coffee+roasters+Copenhagen)**.
All enjoyed, all recommendable, all thoroughly documented elsewhere by
people with better cameras.

## The verdict

**Difficulty: 2/5.** The vegan eating is effortless, everyone is kind,
and every café copes with a toddler. The prices are the only boss fight
— counter them with fair-priced table tennis, strategic brunches, and
enough Gaza Grill hummus to make the whole trip mathematically
justifiable.
