Some trips are built around food, some around a football match. This one
was built around museums — and Stockholm turned out to be almost
suspiciously good at them.

We went Thursday to Sunday in March. Still chilly, but warm enough to
walk the city properly, and the light at that time of year does
something flattering to all that water. The transport is genuinely
superb: everything connects, everything runs, and nothing about moving
around with a small child felt like a problem.

## Where we stayed

**[Scandic Malmen](https://maps.google.com/?q=Scandic+Malmen+Stockholm)**,
in the middle of Södermalm — our second Scandic on this blog, after
[Copenhagen](/blog/copenhagen-with-a-tiny-vegan/).

Honest verdict: the area is busy, a bit dirty, and loud, which is a
real consideration with a family. It is also hustling and bustling,
full of clubs, restaurants and bars, and alive in a way quiet
neighbourhoods aren't. If the noise doesn't bother you, it's a great
base. If you need silence after bedtime, book elsewhere.

## The museums, which were the whole point

### Junibacken

**[Junibacken](https://maps.google.com/?q=Junibacken+Stockholm)** is the
nicest children's museum I have ever seen. Not "nicest we've been to
with a toddler" — nicest, full stop. It's built out of Swedish
children's literature, so you walk through storybooks at child scale:
houses you can enter, worlds drawn rather than built, a level of care
that most attractions aimed at four-year-olds never bother with.

![A hand-drawn storybook house at Junibacken, with a doorway children can walk through](/images/stockholm/junibacken-indoor.jpg)
*Illustrations you can walk into. The whole place is like this.*

![The outdoor play area at Junibacken — a blue tower with a tube slide, fairy-tale houses and strings of coloured lights](/images/stockholm/junibacken-outdoor.jpg)
*And then there's outside.*

### The Museum of Technology

**[Tekniska — the Museum of Technology](https://maps.google.com/?q=Tekniska+Museet+Stockholm)**
is the hands-on kind: build things, pull levers, race things. Luca —
three years and nine months at the time — spent a long stretch
constructing a wooden tower with the focus of a structural engineer,
and another discovering that a ski simulator will let you do downhill
slalom indoors, repeatedly, at no additional charge.

![A small child on a ski simulator machine, skiing a slalom course on screen](/images/stockholm/tekniska-ski.jpg)
*Downhill slalom, indoors, on repeat.*

### The Viking Museum and the shipwreck

The **[Viking Museum](https://maps.google.com/?q=The+Viking+Museum+Stockholm)**
and the **[Vasa](https://maps.google.com/?q=Vasa+Museum+Stockholm)** —
the shipwreck museum — were both excellent, and both work far better
with a small child than their subject matter suggests. A giant salvaged
warship in a dark hall is exactly the sort of thing that silences a
four-year-old mid-sentence, which is its own kind of value.

## The food situation (an unexpected solution)

Here's the thing we didn't expect: **we mostly ate in museum cafés, and
they all had vegan options.** Every single one. In most cities the
museum café is where a vegan family goes to eat a bag of crisps and
regret things; in Stockholm it quietly solved the problem for us four
days running. It also means your eating fits the day instead of
interrupting it — no cross-city hunt at the exact moment everyone's
energy runs out.

And the **cardamom buns** are, as advertised, outstanding. A thread that
began in Prague and escalated in
[Copenhagen](/blog/copenhagen-with-a-tiny-vegan/) reached its logical
conclusion here, at the source. No notes. No complaints. No leftovers.

![A small child holding a pearl-sugared cardamom bun in a café](/images/stockholm/cardamom-bun.jpg)
*The homeland.*

For coffee proper, we had
**[Café Pascal](https://maps.google.com/?q=Caf%C3%A9+Pascal+S%C3%B6dermalm+Stockholm)**
in Södermalm and
**[Beck Kaffebar](https://maps.google.com/?q=Beck+Kaffebar+Stockholm)** —
both the real thing, in a city that takes the subject seriously.

## The city in between

Stockholm rewards the walking you do between museums. **Gamla Stan**,
the old town, is all narrow lanes that open suddenly onto water, and in
March you get it without the summer crush.

![A cobbled street in Gamla Stan sloping down between ochre buildings to the water](/images/stockholm/gamla-stan-water.jpg)
*Every other street ends in the sea.*

And the city keeps handing children things to do for free. In the middle
of **Sergels torg**, a giant stack of wooden crates had been assembled
into a climbing structure — no ticket, no queue, just a pile of
engineering in a public square and a small boy immediately at the top
of it.

![A large climbing structure built from wooden crates in a public square, with children climbing on it](/images/stockholm/sergels-crates.jpg)

## The verdict

**Difficulty: 1/5.** Superb transport, world-class museums for children,
and vegan food where you actually are rather than where you have to
trek to. The only caveat is the one above: pick your neighbourhood with
your tolerance for nightlife in mind.

We will absolutely be going again.
