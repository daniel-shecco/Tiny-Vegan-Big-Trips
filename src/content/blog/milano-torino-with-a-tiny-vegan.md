Full disclosure before we begin: this trip had an agenda. I have been a
Juventus fan since I was eight years old, and when the fixture list
produced Juventus–Atalanta on a weekend we could actually travel, the
family holiday assembled itself around it with suspicious speed.

So: a long weekend in September, Friday to Monday. Fly into Milano, one
night there, train to Torino, football, home. Two Italian cities, four
days, one four-year-old (and three months, he would want me to add),
and — as it turned out — two completely different experiences.

## Milano: beautiful, and a lot

The **[Duomo](https://maps.google.com/?q=Duomo+di+Milano)** is genuinely
breathtaking, and there is a designer shop on every corner in case you
forget where you are. But the honest headline is that Milano has a
*stressful* vibe. People everywhere, narrow streets, and a general
sense of being slightly in the way. With a stroller it never quite
stopped feeling like rush hour.

Credit where it's due: **public transport is solid and properly
stroller-accessible**, which took the edge off. We stayed in an Airbnb
on the edge of the centre near **Crocetta metro station** — handy, calm,
and a short ride from everything.

## The vegan problem in Milano

This is the part we'd want to read before going, so here it is plainly:
**Milano was the hardest city we've done for vegan food.**

Harder than that, it was *misleadingly* hard. We'd check Google, find
restaurants where reviewers explicitly said there were vegan dishes,
turn up, and discover this was simply false. More than once we ended up
going door to door asking restaurants directly, where the concept itself
seemed to be a struggle. If [London's problem](/blog/london-with-a-tiny-vegan/)
was abundance-but-all-chains, Milano's is the opposite: scarcity, with
bad information on top.

Two places saved us:

- **[Pasticceria Nepà](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pasticceria+Nep%C3%A0+Milano)**
  — 100% plant-based, and an amazing café-bakery. After a day of
  negotiating, walking into somewhere where *everything* is available to
  you is a genuine relief. The croissants deserve their reputation.
- **[Flower Burger](https://maps.google.com/?q=Flower+Burger+Milano)** —
  the colourful vegan burger place. It was okay rather than revelatory,
  but given the alternative it absolutely did the job.

![A chocolate-filled croissant held up in two small hands at Pasticceria Nepà, Milano, over an Italia football shirt](/images/milano-torino/nepa-croissant.jpg)
*Nepà. Note the matchday attire, chosen independently and worn for three days.*

## The train, and a magnificent station

We travelled to Torino from
**[Milano Centrale](https://maps.google.com/?q=Milano+Centrale)**, which
is worth arriving early for — it's a magnificent building in its own
right, less a station than a marble cathedral that happens to contain
trains. I booked with **Trenitalia online in advance**; the trip was
smooth, comfortable, and exactly the kind of travel that works with a
small child.

## Torino: the opposite of Milano

Torino is *chill*. The streets are wider and cleaner, nobody is in a
hurry, and — the headline for us — **vegan options were easy to find**.
The whole city runs at a speed that suits travelling with a kid.

We stayed in an Airbnb near **Porta Susa**, which made getting around
effortless.

### The eating

**[Pizzium](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pizzium+Torino+Duchessa+Jolanda)**
— an amazing pizza, vegan mozzarella and all, and the kind of place
where nobody blinks at a small person reaching across the table
mid-slice.

![A pizza with artichokes and vegan mozzarella at Pizzium, Torino, with a small hand arriving from the right](/images/milano-torino/pizzium.jpg)
*Pizzium. The hand belongs to the co-author of this blog.*

**[miscusi](https://maps.google.com/?q=miscusi+pasta+fresca+Porta+Susa+Torino)**
(Porta Susa) — the vegan pasta was delicious. Fair warning: their vegan
choices are very limited, so don't arrive expecting a menu to browse.
Order the one, enjoy the one.

![A bowl of fresh tagliatelle in tomato sauce at miscusi, Torino](/images/milano-torino/miscusi.jpg)
*miscusi: one vegan option, executed properly.*

**[Orso Laboratorio del Caffè](https://maps.google.com/?q=Orso+Laboratorio+del+Caff%C3%A8+Torino)**
— vegan pastries and proper coffee. The pastry-and-espresso stop we
spent all of Milano wishing for.

**[Vanilla](https://maps.google.com/?q=Vanilla+gelato+Torino)** — really
good vegan gelato. I had pistachio, Luca had mango, and you can taste
the quality of the ingredients in both. This is the gelato bar the rest
of Italy keeps promising.

**['skjuma Craft Beers](https://maps.google.com/?q=%27skjuma+Craft+Beers+Torino)**
— cosy and genuine, with none of the polish-over-substance you get in
craft beer bars that are mostly a brand. Good beer, warm room, actual
people. Exactly what you want at the end of a long day of walking.

### Parco del Valentino

A genuine gem. We cycled around the park, and Luca ended up playing
football with local children — the kind of afternoon you cannot plan,
book or pay for, and the one we'll remember longest.

**[Baby Luna Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Baby+Luna+Park+Torino)**
is in the park too — a small, sweet fairground pitched exactly at this
age group, dragons and all.

![The castle-themed entrance arch of Baby Luna Park in Parco del Valentino, Torino](/images/milano-torino/baby-luna-park.jpg)

### The city centre

Torino's centre is beautiful in a way that sneaks up on you: wander far
enough in any direction and you run into something ancient, like the
Roman towers of the
**[Porta Palatina](https://maps.google.com/?q=Porta+Palatina+Torino)**,
standing in the middle of the city as if it were perfectly normal.

![The Roman brick towers and arches of the Porta Palatina in Torino](/images/milano-torino/porta-palatina.jpg)

## Matchday

The highlight, and the reason we came.

On matchday the city fills with jerseys — you see Juventus shirts
everywhere, hours before kickoff, and it is impossible not to get
swept up in it. We left three hours early and the bus to the stadium was
packed solid, which I'd recommend budgeting for.

The **[Juventus Museum](https://maps.google.com/?q=Juventus+Museum+Torino)**
was nice, though expensive for what is essentially a hall-of-fame
exhibition. Luckily there's plenty around
**[Allianz Stadium](https://maps.google.com/?q=Allianz+Stadium+Torino)**
to fill the time: a shopping mall with a food court next door, and
pre-game events inside the ground itself. Three hours that could have
been an ordeal with a small child instead flew by.

![Shirts of Baggio, Sivori, Zidane, Buffon, Parola and Rossi on display at the Juventus Museum](/images/milano-torino/juventus-museum.jpg)

![The Curva Sud at Allianz Stadium before kickoff, full of flags and banners](/images/milano-torino/allianz-stadium.jpg)
*Three hours early. Worth every minute.*

And then Juventus won, which is the correct way to end a pilgrimage.

## The verdict

This one needs two scores.

**Milano — Difficulty: 4/5.** Beautiful, exhausting, and genuinely
difficult as a vegan family: the food information you'll find online is
unreliable, and you will do real work to eat. Transport is excellent and
the Duomo is worth the trip, but one night felt like the right dose.

**Torino — Difficulty: 1/5.** Chill, clean, walkable, easy to eat in,
and full of parks, gelato and Roman ruins. Add a stadium full of people
singing and you have our favourite Italian city so far.

We've already agreed we're going back to Torino.
