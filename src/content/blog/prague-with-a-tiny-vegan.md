Prague's reputation is pork knuckle and beer, which makes its vegan scene
one of Europe's best-kept secrets. The catch: those postcard cobblestones
are a stroller's natural enemy.

## Where to stay

Skip Old Town — beautiful, but it's wall-to-wall tour groups and the
cobbles will rattle a napping toddler awake in minutes. We based ourselves
in **Vinohrady**: leafy, flat-ish, full of specialty coffee, and Riegrovy
Sady park has a playground with a view of the castle.

## The eating shortlist

- **Breakfast:** Vinohrady's third-wave cafés almost all default to oat
  milk without the sigh. Toddler verdict: approved via the cinnamon bun.
- **Lunch:** the veggie canteen format is everywhere — point at what you
  want, pay by weight. Pointing is the toddler ordering system, so this
  city is basically built for us.
- **Dinner:** several fully vegan Czech restaurants do plant-based
  versions of svíčková and goulash. Order the dumplings. Always the
  dumplings.

## Supermarket cheat sheet

Every Albert and Billa has a decent plant-based fridge. The
accidentally-vegan local win: **rohlíky** — the ubiquitous bread rolls,
about 4 koruna each, and the single greatest toddler-silencing device in
Central Europe.

## The verdict

**Difficulty: 2/5.** Amazing value, great food, kind grandmas everywhere.
Bring a carrier for the castle hill and Old Town; save the stroller for
Vinohrady and the riverbank.
