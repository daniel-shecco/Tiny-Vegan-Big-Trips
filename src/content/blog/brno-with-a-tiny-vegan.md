Brno is charming, small, walkable and beautiful — and then it quietly
produces attractions and food that don't fall short of a major
cosmopolitan capital. We came for a short weekend and left slightly
annoyed that we hadn't booked longer.

It's also the easiest kind of trip from Vienna: a train, a short walk,
and you're in the middle of it. If you've read our
[Prague guide](/blog/prague-with-a-tiny-vegan/), think of Brno as its
smaller, calmer sibling — same excellent value, none of the crowds.

## Where we stayed

**[Hotel International Brno](https://maps.google.com/?q=Hotel+International+Brno)**
— very central, and within walking distance of essentially everything
in the city center, which with a small child is worth more than any
amenity list. Right next door are the **Špilberk** castle gardens,
where we took one of the loveliest walks of the trip roughly forty
metres from our own front door.

## The coffee situation

Brno's superpower: **almost everywhere you go, the coffee is good.**
Not "fine for a small city" good — actually good. We stopped drinking
espresso defensively and started ordering it with confidence.

- **[SKØG](https://maps.google.com/?q=SK%C3%98G+Brno)** — the food
  stop. A properly excellent kimchi–cheddar vegan sandwich, and a vegan
  open focaccia that arrived looking like it had been styled for a
  magazine and tasted better than that implies.
- **[qb coffee roasters](https://maps.google.com/?q=qb+coffee+roasters+Brno)**
  — down by the river, with a summer vibe that stuck with us longer
  than most museums do.
- **[Pikola espresso bar](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pikola+espresso+bar+Brno)**
  — small, sharp, exactly what you want mid-wander.

## The babyccino heist

**[CAFÉ MITTE](https://maps.google.com/?q=Caf%C3%A9+Mitte+Brno)** gets
its own heading for one detail: they served Luca his babyccino in a
rubber cup convincingly disguised as a real clay coffee cup. Luca —
a few days short of four, and taking the milestone seriously —
inspected it, accepted it as proof of adulthood, and immediately
ordered another. Total cost of the ruse: one extra babyccino. Worth
every crown.

It's also where we spent our last hours. We'd checked out, seen what we
came to see, and had a train to wait for — so we sat in the garden
playing cards until it was time to go. If you're going to kill an hour
somewhere, kill it there.

## BRuNO family park

Enormous, indoors *and* out, and it consumed four hours without anyone
noticing. The consequence arrived on schedule: a child so thoroughly
spent that bedtime came early and without negotiation. If you take one
thing from this guide, take
**[this one](https://maps.google.com/?q=BRuNO+family+park+Brno)**.

## The pools

**[The aquapark](https://maps.google.com/?q=STAREZ+Aquapark+Kohoutovice+Brno)**
was nice, pleasant, and fairly cheap compared to Therme Wien. Smaller,
yes, but we had a genuinely lovely afternoon there, and the price
difference is not subtle.

## Ice cream, with an asterisk

**[JEŠTĚ jednu](https://maps.google.com/?q=JE%C5%A0T%C4%9A+jednu+Brno)**
makes great homemade ice cream — but only the sorbets are vegan, which
is a small heartbreak in an otherwise obliging city. Go anyway: the
sorbets are worth the trip, and a child does not care about the
distinction even slightly.

## Been there, had fun — but you've read about these in ten other blogs

We also did the **[VIDA! science centre](https://maps.google.com/?q=VIDA!+science+centre+Brno)**
and **[Jungle Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Jungle+Park+Brno)**, the
high-ropes course. Jungle Park sits a bit outside the center, but
nothing a thirty-minute bus or tram ride can't solve — and in Brno,
that still counts as nearby.

## The verdict

**Difficulty: 1/5.** Small, walkable, beautiful, cheap by Western
European standards, and stacked with things a child will happily
destroy an afternoon on. Give it **three or four days** and treat it as
a relaxing holiday rather than a city-break sprint — Brno rewards the
slower version of itself.
